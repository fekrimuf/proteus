## Calibration of the hours

An agent-hour is one agent session's wall-clock hour, tests and guide included. Measured on this repository's own history: between the commits of 2026-10-12 23:10 and 2026-10-13 01:15 the capabilities 1 to 6 of [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) B18 landed and the tree family began, about two hours for six capabilities, 0.3 hours each. The plan dates in B12.6 and B18 were set at one gated step per working day, which is five to ten times slower than what the history shows, so the dates below are re-set from the measured pace. The planning basis is three times the measured pace: 1 hour for a small or medium step, 3 hours for a large one, plus the owner's turnaround where the owner's eye is the gate. Renders are limited by the machine, not the agent: about ten minutes a frame at owner quality, one at a time.

## One row at a time (the queue)

One agent session works at a time, on one row, in a new session per row ([DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) B24). The three lanes of 2026-10-13 ran for forty minutes before the plan's five-hour usage limit stopped every agent mid-work: the allowance is one pool, so agents side by side spend it faster without adding work to the window. Run one after another, the scheduled rows take about seven to fifteen working days; that is the plan basis. The only dependencies are 4 after 5 (the building's shape needs no new material: it uses the scanned wood until the thatch and the worn ground arrive), 5a after 5 and 4 (rooms need the building and its materials) and 7 after 5 (the sites are for buildings).

**The queue, biggest lever first:**

1. **Row 1a**: done and merged 2026-10-13 (LOG, last entry). Row 9's buried instances are dropped by the same rule.
2. **Row 5, first slice: the building shell**: done 2026-10-13 (LOG, last entry); the cottage replaces the plank hut in the creek world.
3. **Rows 1 and 2:** owner-quality frames of the creek, then light and air. These give the owner's side-by-side look against the reference.
4. **Row 5, the rest:** the mill and the water wheel (the hut is the weakest thing in the frames the owner judges good).
5. **Row 3** (nature that grows), **row 2a** (the engine look), **row 4** (materials), **row 6** (style tools), **row 7** (scene composer), **row 5a** (interiors), **row 8** (USD export, before an engine handoff), and the agent-errors half of row 9.

**How a row runs.** A new session on Sonnet 5.5 at medium effort (the owner's rule, 2026-10-13: rows are built on Sonnet medium; planning, route changes, research, discussion with the owner and the review of a finished row are done on Opus 5.5 at high or extra-high effort, in a short session of their own). The row has a written brief before it starts: what to build, the test that must fail without it, what the picture must show. The session works on a branch named for the row with its own build folder (`CARGO_TARGET_DIR`, because the editor's tool server holds the main checkout's `pt.exe` open), makes a checkpoint commit at every passing test run, makes and reviews its own pictures, and ends with the gate and the wrap-up. The executor builds exactly the brief and does not look past it (measured on the certificate, LOG 2026-10-13), so the brief and the review carry the quality.

**What a row costs in allowance (measured 2026-10-13, tokens read, fresh Sonnet sessions):** the heavy-job lock 3 million, the certificate fix 5 million, row 1a to about two thirds 12 million, the building shell's first slice with one fix round begun 19 million. The five-hour window let about 107 million through in all before the limit, of which two Opus sessions and the Opus lead took 68 million.

**Kept from the lanes:** the heavy-job lock (only one render, bake, engine launch or judge runs on the machine at a time, across `pt` processes and worktrees; [ENVIRONMENT.md](ENVIRONMENT.md)); the cloud session as a place for a code-only row (4 processors, 16 GB, Linux, no graphics card; the owner starts it at claude.ai/code with the brief, and it draws on the same allowance; the first trial built, tested and pushed the certificate in under twenty minutes); and the measured fact that an executor the lead starts as "remote" runs on this PC. Lanes side by side come back unchanged when the allowance is larger, which is a spend and the owner's decision.

**How the blind pairs run** (delayed, DECISIONS B20; they use views the owner has not seen, never the creek views already shown; originally the stage 1 gate, [PRO_QUALITY_PLAN.md](PRO_QUALITY_PLAN.md) B12.6): ten slides, each two pictures of the same view side by side, one made by our rules and one set up by hand from the same assets, same camera and same sun; which is which is hidden and the sides are shuffled. For each slide the owner answers one question: which is closer to the reference picture. The gate is passed when the owner picks ours in at least 5 of 10. A miss changes the approach for that stage, and the owner's one-line reason per slide says what to fix.
