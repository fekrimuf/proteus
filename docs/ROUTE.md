## Calibration of the hours

An agent-hour is one agent session's wall-clock hour, tests and guide included. Measured on this repository's own history: between the commits of 2026-10-12 23:10 and 2026-10-13 01:15 the capabilities 1 to 6 of [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) B18 landed and the tree family began, about two hours for six capabilities, 0.3 hours each. The plan dates in B12.6 and B18 were set at one gated step per working day, which is five to ten times slower than what the history shows, so the dates below are re-set from the measured pace. The planning basis is three times the measured pace: 1 hour for a small or medium step, 3 hours for a large one, plus the owner's turnaround where the owner's eye is the gate. Renders are limited by the machine, not the agent: about ten minutes a frame at owner quality, one at a time.

## Lanes

Run one after another, the scheduled rows take about seven to fifteen working days. Their only dependencies are 4 after 5 (the building's shape needs no new material: it uses the scanned wood until the thatch and the worn ground arrive), 5a after 5 and 4 (rooms need the building and its materials) and 7 after 5 (the sites are for buildings), so three lanes can run side by side, each in its own git worktree with its own build folder, with the gate ([TOOLS.md](TOOLS.md)) as the merge test and the command registry keeping the lanes out of each other's files:

* **Lane A (the critical path to the owner's side-by-side look):** rows 1a, 1, 2 and 2a, then 8, 9 and 15.
* **Lane B (nature, style and interiors):** rows 3, then 6, then 5a once rows 5 and 4 have merged.
* **Lane C (buildings and surfaces):** rows 5, then 4, then 7. The mill comes before the materials (changed 2026-10-13: the hut is the weakest thing in the frames the owner judges good).

The longest lane is B (21 to 41 hours), so three lanes bring the scheduled rows to about three to six working days. One job that takes the GPU or several GB of memory runs at a time, whichever lane asks.

**Which model does which job (the owner's rule, 2026-10-13):** a lane is executed on Sonnet 5.5 at medium effort; planning, route changes, research and discussion with the owner are done on Opus 5.5 at high or extra-high effort. So the lead writes a brief for each row before its lane starts (what to build, the test that must fail without it, what the picture must show) and reviews the result before the merge.

**Before the lanes start (about 1 hour, the first job on the owner's word):** today the one-heavy-job rule is kept inside each `pt` process, not across lanes: `lowload` caps the children of one `pt` at `PT_HEAVY_MEM_GB`, so three lanes could hold three capped jobs at once on this 16 GB machine with its one 8 GB card. The route: a machine-wide lock in `lowload` that makes a second render, bake, engine launch or judge wait its turn, with a test that fails without it; each lane's build limited to two of the eight cores (`CARGO_BUILD_JOBS=2`); lanes drive `pt` from the command line and do not start a second tool server on port 7700. **The owner approved the three lanes on 2026-10-13** (the extra use of the agent allowance, no cash spend). The lanes started on the owner's word the same day. Executors the lead starts run on this PC, each in its own git worktree with its own build folder (measured 2026-10-13: the lead's "remote" start fell back to a local worktree, so three builds ran here at once). A cloud session (4 processors, 16 GB, Linux, no graphics card) keeps a lane's builds off the owner's machine, and the owner starts it at claude.ai/code with the lead's brief: code and tests there, and the pictures of a row are made on this PC when its branch comes back, before the merge. The first cloud trial (the certificate, row 15) built, tested and pushed in under twenty minutes.

A miss changes the approach for that stage, and the owner's one-line reason per slide says what to fix.
